Chapter 14: Work and Potential Energy

In the previous chapters we have studied the laws of motion and the idea of energy. We found that the conservation of energy is a very powerful principle. It tells us that if a system is isolated, its total energy cannot change.

Now we want to investigate the relationship between force and energy. Suppose a particle moves along a curve from point A to point B under the influence of a force $\mathbf{F}$. We define the work done by the force as the integral of the tangential component of the force along the path:

W = ∫ $\mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{s}$

This definition is motivated by the observation that when a force acts on an object in the direction of its motion, it increases the object's kinetic energy. If the force opposes the motion, the kinetic energy decreases.
